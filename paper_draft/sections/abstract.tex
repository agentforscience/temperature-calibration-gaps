Softmax temperature is the most widely exposed decoding setting, and practitioners choose it by folklore: 0.2 for code, 0.7 for chat, above 1 for creativity. We ask how output diversity and quality change with temperature on open-ended, factual, and coding tasks, and whether the useful range depends on model size and family. We sample 10 open-weight models from four families at 11 temperatures from 0 to 2.0 on stories, closed-book trivia, and executed code, for about 277,000 samples including two control runs. Quality does not decline gradually. It stays nearly flat and then falls at a point specific to each model: story coherence drops below 90\% of peak at $T \approx 0.84$ for Qwen2.5-0.5B and $T \approx 1.31$ for Qwen2.5-14B. Code does not collapse just above 0.8 as folklore suggests: no model drops significantly from 0.6 to 0.8, and models of 3B and larger in the Qwen family, plus Gemma, stay within 90\% of peak to $T \approx 1.1$--$1.4$. Factual accuracy loses only 0.7--5.2 points at $T$ of 0.8--1.0, with no further coverage gain. Larger models tolerate higher temperature on all three tasks in the Qwen family, but only on QA for Llama, and Llama curves differ from the others by 4--7 times the seed noise floor. Temperatures below about 0.8 are safe on nearly every model tested but do little for diversity; above 1.0, the right setting has to be checked per model.
