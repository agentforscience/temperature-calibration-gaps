# Temperature Calibration Gaps: When Softmax Temperature Fails to Control Output Diversity

## 1. Executive Summary

**Question.** How do output diversity and quality change with softmax temperature (T = 0–2.0) on open-ended, factual
and coding tasks, and does the useful range depend on model size and family?

**Main finding.** Temperature does not fail gradually. Quality stays almost flat up to a model-specific point and then
drops sharply. That point depends strongly on the model: story coherence falls below 90% of peak at T ≈ 0.84 for
Qwen2.5-0.5B and T ≈ 1.31 for Qwen2.5-14B. The hypothesis's fixed thresholds (0.7, 0.8, 1.2) describe no single model
well, and the coding clause is largely wrong for models of 3B and up.

| Clause | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Open-ended: diversity plateaus above 1.2, coherence drops 20–40% | **Partly supported** | Measured diversity stops rising in 9/10 models, but it peaks at T = 1.0–1.4 and then falls. Coherence loss is usually larger than predicted: 48–63% at T = 1.4 in 9/10 models |
| Factual QA: above 0.7 accuracy degrades with no meaningful diversity gain | **Mostly supported, small effect** | T = 0.8–1.0 is worse than T ≤ 0.7 in 9/10 models, but only by 0.7–5.2 points. Coverage over 4 samples gains nothing further above 0.7 |
| Code: narrow useful range 0.2–0.6, sharp collapse above 0.8 | **Refuted as stated** | No model drops significantly from 0.6 to 0.8. Greedy (T = 0) is best or tied-best for 5/10 models, and models ≥3B in the Qwen family and Gemma stay within 90% of peak to T ≈ 1.1–1.4 |
| Smaller models have narrower useful ranges | **Supported for Qwen, weak for Llama** | Qwen ladder: yes on all three tasks. Llama ladder: QA only; story cliff does not move from 1B to 8B |
| Curve shape differs across families at the same scale | **Supported** | Llama differs from the other three families by 4–7× the seed noise floor; Qwen and Mistral are close to each other |

**Practical implication.** Below T ≈ 0.8 temperature is safe on nearly every model tested, but it is a weak diversity
control. Above T ≈ 1.0, check the specific model: identical settings can give intact output on one model and word
salad on another of the same size.

No API key was available, so all subject models are open-weight checkpoints run locally. GPT, Claude and Gemini were
not tested and these results should not be assumed to apply to them.

## 2. Research Question & Hypothesis

Testable sub-hypotheses (H1–H5) are defined in `planning.md`:

- **H1 open-ended:** diversity gain is much smaller above T = 1.2, while coherence at T ≥ 1.4 is 20–40% below peak.
- **H2 factual QA:** accuracy above 0.7 falls, with little gain in coverage.
- **H3 code:** useful range ≈ 0.2–0.6, sharp collapse above 0.8.
- **H4 size:** the upper end of the useful range grows with model size.
- **H5 family:** curves differ across families at matched size.

## 3. Literature Context

The resource-finding stage produced no `literature_review.md`, `papers/` or `code/`. The works below are cited **from
memory and were not re-checked against the papers**, so treat their details as unverified:

- Holtzman et al. (2020): degeneration at high randomness motivated nucleus sampling.
- Chen et al. (2021, Codex): best pass@1 at low T, best pass@k at moderate T. Our pass@4 pattern is consistent with this.
- Renze & Guven (2024): accuracy broadly flat over T in [0, 1].
- Peeperkorn et al. (2024): temperature only weakly raises story novelty and costs coherence.

The intended contribution is one controlled grid across tasks, sizes and families, extending to T = 2.0.

## 4. Methodology

### Models
Ten models, plus two controls. All use pure temperature sampling (`top_p=1`, `top_k` off, `min_p=0`,
`repetition_penalty=1`, `generation_config="vllm"`), with vLLM 0.32 on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs.

| Key | Checkpoint | Params | Precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen2.5-0.5b / -1.5b | Qwen/Qwen2.5-{0.5,1.5}B-Instruct | 0.5B / 1.5B | bf16 |
| qwen2.5-3b / -7b / -14b | Qwen/Qwen2.5-{3,7,14}B-Instruct-AWQ | 3.1B / 7.6B / 14.8B | AWQ-INT4 |
| llama3.2-1b | unsloth/Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct | 1.2B | bf16 |
| llama3.2-3b | AMead10/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct-AWQ | 3.2B | AWQ-INT4 |
| llama3.1-8b | hugging-quants/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct-AWQ-INT4 | 8.0B | AWQ-INT4 |
| mistral-7b | solidrust/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3-AWQ | 7.2B | AWQ-INT4 |
| gemma2-9b | hugging-quants/gemma-2-9b-it-AWQ-INT4 | 9.2B | AWQ-INT4 |
| *control:* qwen2.5-0.5b-seed2 | same as 0.5B, different sampling seeds | 0.5B | bf16 |
| *control:* qwen2.5-7b-bf16 | Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct | 7.6B | bf16 |

### Design
- **Temperatures:** 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 2.0.
- **Samples:** 4 per (prompt, T); 1 at T = 0, which is deterministic. That is 23,124 samples per run and about 277,000
  in total across 12 runs. All 12 runs completed all 6,204 (prompt, T) cells.
- **Stories:** 100 WritingPrompts test prompts, up to 256 tokens.
- **Factual QA:** 300 closed-book TriviaQA validation questions, up to 24 tokens.
- **Code:** all 164 HumanEval problems, up to 448 tokens, executed against the official tests in a resource-limited
  subprocess. Full prompts are in `src/prep_data.py`.

### Metrics
- **Stories, diversity:** mean pairwise cosine distance of `all-mpnet-base-v2` embeddings (`sem_div`), and distinct-2
  over pooled samples.
- **Stories, quality:** Qwen3-8B judge, 1–9, probability-weighted digit, on 2 of 4 samples; "coherent rate" = share scoring ≥5; lexical validity.
- **QA:** per-sample accuracy (normalized alias match), coverage@4 (any of 4 correct), distinct answers.
- **Code:** pass@1, pass@4, syntax validity, distinct programs by AST.
- **Useful range:** temperatures with quality ≥ 90% of the model's peak, linearly interpolated; T_u is the upper
  bound. Story quality here is the coherent rate.

All 25,200 judgments (2,100 per run) passed the logprob validity checks, with none falling back to text parsing.

### Statistics
Item-level bootstrap (2,000 resamples) for CIs, paired sign-flip permutation tests with Holm correction, and Spearman
correlation for size trends. Curve-shape gaps are compared with two measured floors: a seed replicate and a
bf16-vs-AWQ control.

### Changes from the plan
- **14B and 3B models moved to AWQ.** Shared-GPU memory ruled out 14B bf16, and 3B moved to fit a small idle slice.
  Precision therefore co-varies with size in both ladders (bf16 ≤1.5B, AWQ ≥3B). Section 5.6 shows the effect on curve
  shape is small but the effect on level is not zero.
- **Story useful range uses the coherent rate, not the raw judge score.** This was decided after seeing Qwen2.5-0.5B
  and Llama-1B results, because the judge rarely scores below 3.
- **The judge rubric was edited once** after inspecting five samples, before any scored judging run.
- **A seed replicate was added** as a noise floor, and Holm correction and ladder contrasts were added to the analysis.
  Several analysis choices were therefore made after seeing partial data.

- **Plateau:** supported in the sense that diversity stops improving, but "plateau" is the wrong shape. Semantic
  diversity rises, peaks and falls in 9/10 models. Qwen2.5-14B is the exception, with no slowdown between the two
  ranges (ratio 0.98, 95% CI 0.69–1.30).
- **Most of the "plateau" is measurement.** Distinct-2 flattens because it reaches its ceiling of 1.0. The fall in

  less varied.
- **Diversity among coherent outputs keeps rising.** Restricted to prompts where both judged samples are coherent,
  `sem_div` increases at almost every T until too few coherent groups remain
  (`results/analysis/sem_div_coherent_groups.csv`). This is a selected subset, but it suggests coherence loss, not
  saturation, is what ends useful diversity.
- **Coherence:** the 20–40% prediction is met only in passing. At T = 1.4 one model falls in or near that range
  (14B, 19%); nine lose 48–63%. Because the judge's floor is about 3, these figures understate the damage, and the
  raw scale cannot fall more than about 64%. The coherent rate shows it better: it goes from about 1.0 to about 0 within
  one or two grid steps.

## 6. Limitations

- **One judge, compressed scale.** The Qwen3-8B judge rarely scores below 3, and it shares a lineage with the Qwen
  subjects. A manual audit of 16 boundary stories agreed with the ≥5 threshold on 12 (75%), with the judge slightly
  conservative. That audit was by the researcher-agent, on one model, and is small (`results/judge_audit.md`).
- **The embedding metric is misleading on degenerate text,** as described in 5.1.
- **Family and checkpoint are confounded,** and precision co-varies with size.
- **Mistral's low code score** (.268) is below what its QA score would suggest. Restoring prompt imports raised it
  only to .280, and failures are mostly real assertion errors, but the cause is unexplained. It may be specific to
  this checkpoint or prompt, so its code level should not be taken as representative of Mistral-7B.
- **Scope.** Pure temperature only, with no top-p or top-k. Tests cover short stories, one prompt per task, and 4
  samples per cell. T = 0 uses one sample, and a single seed replicate is a thin noise floor. vLLM batch composition can
  also change outputs slightly.
- **Grid resolution.** Steps of 0.2 above T = 0.8 limit how precisely a cliff can be placed, and T_u depends on the
  90% threshold choice. Bootstrap CIs on story T_u are narrow partly because of this interpolation.
- **Multiple comparisons** were corrected within tests but not across the full set of hypotheses.
- **Contamination:** TriviaQA, HumanEval and WritingPrompts are public and may be in training data. This matters
  little for curve shapes, more for absolute levels.

## 7. Conclusions & Next Steps

Temperature's useful range is set by where each model breaks, and that varies widely. There is no universal threshold,
and the common 0.7 default is safe rather than optimal. Single-attempt code and QA do best at low T, while pass@4 and
coverage@4 benefit from moderate T.

Suggested follow-ups:

1. Measure logit scale and the top-token margin per model to test whether they predict the cliff location.
2. Add more checkpoints per family to separate family from checkpoint.
3. Run hosted frontier models through the same grid when an API key is available.
4. Replace `sem_div` with a measure that does not collapse on degenerate text, and add human coherence ratings.
5. Sample T at 0.05 steps around each model's cliff to test how abrupt it really is.

# Planning: Temperature Calibration Gaps

## Motivation & Novelty Assessment

### Why This Research Matters
Softmax temperature is the single most widely exposed decoding knob, and practitioners pick it by folklore
("0.7 for chat, 0.2 for code, >1 for creativity"). If temperature's effect on *useful* diversity saturates while
quality keeps falling, or if the useful range depends on model size/family, then a single default is miscalibrated
for most deployments. A task × model × temperature map tells practitioners where the knob still does something.

### Gap in Existing Work
No resource-finder output was available for this workspace (no `literature_review.md`, `papers/`, `code/`), so the
gap is stated from prior knowledge: Renze & Guven (2024, "The Effect of Sampling Temperature on Problem Solving in
LLMs") found accuracy on MCQA flat over T∈[0,1] for several models; Peeperkorn et al. (2024, "Is Temperature the
Creativity Parameter of LLMs?") found only weak novelty gains and coherence loss with temperature for one model on
story writing; Holtzman et al. (2020) motivated nucleus sampling by degeneration at high T; Chen et al. (2021,
Codex) reported best pass@1 at low T and best pass@100 at ~0.8. These studies each look at one task type, few models,
and rarely above T=1.0, and none jointly measures diversity *and* quality curves across a size ladder and several
families with matched prompts and decoding settings.

### Our Novel Contribution
One controlled grid: 10 open-weight instruct models (two size ladders: Qwen2.5 0.5–14B, Llama-3 1–8B; plus Mistral-7B
and Gemma-2-9B at ~7–9B) × 11 temperatures (0–2.0, pure temperature sampling, top-p=1) × 3 task types (open-ended
stories, closed-book factual QA, code with execution). For each cell we measure diversity (semantic + lexical) and
quality (LLM-judge coherence + model-independent proxies, QA accuracy, pass@1), and derive per-model "useful
temperature ranges" and curve-shape parameters to test each clause of the hypothesis.

### Experiment Justification
- Exp 1 (stories, WritingPrompts): tests the "diversity plateaus above 1.2 while coherence drops 20–40%" clause.
- Exp 2 (TriviaQA closed-book): tests "above 0.7 accuracy degrades without meaningful diversity gains"; we measure
  both per-sample accuracy and coverage@4 (the only diversity that is *useful* for factual QA).
- Exp 3 (HumanEval with execution): tests "useful range 0.2–0.6, sharp collapse above 0.8".
- Exp 4 (cross-model analysis on Exps 1–3): useful-range width vs. size (two ladders) → "smaller models have narrower
  useful ranges"; curve-shape comparison at fixed scale (7–9B: Qwen/Llama/Mistral/Gemma; 1–1.5B and 3B: Qwen vs Llama)
  → "curve shape differs across families". A Qwen2.5-7B AWQ run controls for the bf16/AWQ precision confound.

## Research Question
How do output diversity and output quality vary with softmax temperature (0–2.0) across open-ended generation,
factual QA and code generation, and do the useful temperature ranges depend on model size and family?

## Hypothesis Decomposition
- H1 (open-ended): diversity gain per unit T is much smaller above T=1.2 than below it (plateau), while coherence at
  T≥1.4 is 20–40% below its peak.
- H2 (factual QA): accuracy at T∈{0.8..2.0} is significantly lower than at T≤0.7, while coverage@4 (any of 4 samples
  correct) gains little over greedy accuracy.
- H3 (code): pass@1 ≥90% of max only for T in ~[0.2,0.6] (actually: we test whether the useful range upper bound is
  ≈0.6–0.8) and drops sharply (e.g. >30% relative) from T=0.6 to T=1.0.
- H4 (size): the useful-range upper bound T_u (largest T with quality ≥ 90% of the model's best) increases with model
  size within each family.
- H5 (family): at matched scale, temperature–diversity curves (and T_u / T50) differ significantly across families.

Independent variables: temperature, model (size, family), task. Dependent: diversity and quality metrics below.

## Proposed Methodology

### Approach
Real model generations with vLLM 0.32 on local GPUs (no API key is available on this host; the hosted
Qwen3-8B vLLM server on :8011 serves as the LLM judge). Pure temperature sampling (top_p=1, top_k off, min_p=0,
repetition_penalty=1, `generation_config="vllm"` so checkpoint defaults like Qwen's top_p=0.8 are NOT applied).
Temperatures {0, .2, .4, .6, .7, .8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 2.0}; 4 samples per (prompt, T>0), 1 greedy sample at T=0.

### Experimental Steps
1. `prep_data.py`: 100 WritingPrompts, 300 TriviaQA (rc.nocontext val), 164 HumanEval, seed 42.
2. `worker.py`: generate for every model (stories ≤256 tokens, QA ≤24, code ≤448); store text, finish reason and the
   raw (T=1) log-prob of each sampled token.
3. `score.py`: QA accuracy (normalized alias match), HumanEval execution (sandboxed subprocess, 10 s timeout),
   story diversity (mpnet embedding pairwise distance, distinct-2), lexical-validity and self-NLL proxies.
4. `judge.py`: Qwen3-8B judges story coherence/fluency 1–9 (probability-weighted digit, validated logprobs) on 2 of
   the 4 samples per (prompt, T).
5. `analyze.py`: curves, useful ranges, bootstrap CIs, hypothesis tests, figures.

### Baselines
Greedy decoding (T=0) and the conventional T=0.7/1.0 defaults are the reference points; each model is its own
baseline (quality is normalized to the model's best temperature).

### Evaluation Metrics
- Stories: semantic diversity = mean pairwise cosine distance of the 4 samples (all-mpnet-base-v2); lexical diversity
  = distinct-2 over the pooled samples; coherence = judge score 1–9; proxies = fraction of tokens that are dictionary
  words (vocabulary built from the WritingPrompts train split) and mean raw log-prob under the generating model.
- QA: per-sample accuracy, coverage@4, number of distinct normalized answers / 4.
- Code: pass@1 (mean over samples), pass@4, syntactic validity, distinct-program ratio.
- Derived: T_u (largest T with quality ≥ 0.9×best, linear interpolation), T50 (quality = 0.5×best).

### Statistical Analysis Plan
Item-level bootstrap (2,000 resamples) for 95% CIs; paired sign-flip permutation tests on item-level differences
between temperatures (α=0.05, Holm correction within each hypothesis family); Spearman correlation of T_u with
log(params); family differences in T_u/T50 via bootstrap CIs of pairwise differences. Plateau test: ratio of
diversity slope over [1.2,2.0] to slope over [0.2,1.2] with bootstrap CI.

## Expected Outcomes
Support: diversity slope ratio ≪1 above 1.2 with coherence drop 20–40%; QA accuracy drop above 0.7 with coverage@4
≈ greedy accuracy; code T_u≈0.6–0.8; T_u increasing with size; non-overlapping CIs for family curve parameters.
Refute: diversity keeps rising roughly linearly to T=2 (no plateau); coherence collapse much larger (>40%) or later;
QA flat up to 1.0; code flat to 1.0; no size trend.

## Timeline and Milestones
Setup+data 30 min; generation ~3–4 h (shared GPUs, run largest models first on the free GPU); scoring and judging in
parallel as model files complete; analysis 45 min; report 45 min.

## Potential Challenges
- GPU contention (shared host): hold GPU0 memory from the start, run models in one long-lived worker.
- Judge bias (Qwen3 judging Qwen2.5 outputs) → judge-free proxies reported alongside.
- Degenerate high-T outputs inflate lexical/semantic "diversity" → report diversity restricted to coherent samples.
- Precision confound (AWQ for Mistral/Gemma) → Qwen2.5-7B AWQ vs bf16 control.

## Success Criteria
All 10 models × 11 temperatures × 3 tasks generated and scored; each sub-hypothesis gets a supported / refuted /
partially-supported verdict with CIs.
